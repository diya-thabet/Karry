\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document establishes the master technical architecture, domain-driven design (DDD), mathematical formulations, and operational specifications for **Karry** (\textit{Application pour la Gestion de Carri\`ere}), an enterprise Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform engineered for civil engineering extractions, aggregate quarries, sand washing plants, and open-pit mining operations. Karry bridges physical field extractions and executive intelligence through dynamic closed-loop processing graph equations, variable conveyor belt speeds, dual-unit metric conversions ($m^3 \leftrightarrow \text{Tonnes}$), hybrid predictive maintenance tracking (Hours / KM / Calendar fallback), multi-warehouse logistics (\textit{Magasin G\'en\'eral} hub network), two-tier controller verification pipelines, native electronic PKI signatures, cryptographic anti-corruption audit ledgers, interactive visual analytics curves, weather risk delay forecasting, and an executive AI copilot layer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Executive Summary and Strategic Vision}

\subsection{Project Overview}
The **Karry Platform** (\textit{Application pour la Gestion de Carri\`ere}) is an all-in-one enterprise operational operating system for quarrying, aggregate crushing, sand washing, and civil engineering extractions. Built as a progressive web application (PWA) with offline-first capabilities, Karry connects heavy mobile fleets, crushing plants, weighbridges, warehouse networks, shift controllers, and executive financial ledgers into a real-time, tamper-proof intelligence system.

\subsection{Key Industry Vulnerabilities Addressed}
Medium-to-large scale extraction operations globally face nine systemic operational vulnerabilities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Field Data Fraud \& Unverified Fuel Logging}: Manual logging of engine hour meters, diesel dispatches, and scale tickets leads to fuel theft, inflated operational expenses, and falsified shift reports.
    \item \textbf{Fixed Maintenance Metric Limitations}: Traditional maintenance software fails when engine hour meters or odometers break. Karry implements hybrid metric tracking (Engine Hours, Odometer Kilometers, or Calendar Time Fallback with automated 30-day usage switches).
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Conveyor Belt \& Plant Speed Degradation}: Conveyor belt delivery speeds ($\text{m}^3/\text{min}$), screen feeds, and crusher outputs degrade dynamically over time due to mechanical surface wear, belt inclination angles, and wet clay saturation.
    \item \textbf{Unpredicted Wear Component Failures}: High-value components (jaw liners, mantle pairs, polyurethane screen meshes, track shoes) break unexpectedly, causing costly plant shutdowns in remote regions where replacement transit takes weeks.
    \item \textbf{Inaccurate Mass Yields \& Static vs. Closed-Loop Modeling}: Lack of dynamic mass-balance tracking between blasted bench volumes ($V_{\text{blast}}$) and final sellable aggregate fractions ($M_{\text{final}}$) across closed-loop recirculating screens.
    \item \textbf{Paper-Based Bureaucracy \& Lack of Digital Signatures}: Paper signing, scanning, and physical ticket transport slow down shift approvals. Karry embeds native PKI touchscreen electronic signatures with cryptographic SHA-256 verification.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Quarry Warehouse \& Material Leakage}: Lack of historical tracking when spare parts, crusher liners, or heavy machinery move between a Central Warehouse (\textit{Magasin G\'en\'eral}) and satellite site stores.
    \item \textbf{Rigid Load Unit Systems}: Loading operators and weighmaster clerks cannot dynamically toggle between volumetric ($m^3$) and gravimetric ($\text{Tonnes}$) measures during field dispatch.
    \item \textbf{Unmonitored Loan Amortization vs. Weather Risks}: Inability to compare real-time net machinery revenue ($C_{\text{ops}}$) against monthly bank financing costs ($C_{\text{loan}}$), compounded by tropical rainy season shutdowns ($R(m)$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Domain Architecture and Mathematical Foundations}

The platform relies on deterministic mathematical models to govern physical extractions, processing kinetics, wear degradation, fleet financing, and delivery scheduling.

\subsection{Blast Volume Yield, Dynamic Unit Toggle, and Mass Engine}
The in-situ rock volume $V_{\text{rock}}$ ($m^3$) extracted from a bench during a blast operation is calculated via spatial grid parameters:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{rock}} = A_{\text{pattern}} \times h_{\text{bench}} \times \cos(\theta_{\text{slope}})
\end{equation}
Where $A_{\text{pattern}}$ is the drill pattern surface area ($m^2$), $h_{\text{bench}}$ is bench height, and $\theta_{\text{slope}}$ is the bench face angle.

Field operators (loader drivers, scale operators) are provided a **Dynamic Unit Toggle** allowing real-time conversion between volume $V$ ($m^3$) and mass $M$ ($\text{Tonnes}$) using moisture-adjusted rock density:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{raw}} = V_{\text{rock}} \times \rho_{\text{rock}} \times \kappa_{\text{moisture}}
\end{equation}
Where $\rho_{\text{rock}}$ is dry specific gravity ($\text{ton}/m^3$) and $\kappa_{\text{moisture}} \ge 1.0$ accounts for water saturation due to weather.

The overall **Mass Yield Efficiency** ($\eta_{\text{yield}}$) across all commercial aggregate fractions ($i = 1 \dots n$) is:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\text{yield}} = \left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} M_{\text{product}_i}}{M_{\text{raw}}} \right) \times 100\%
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conveyor Belt Dynamic Production Speed \& Plant Physics Engine}
Conveyor belts (\textit{tapis roulants} / \textit{sebta}), screen feeds, and crushers do not run at constant speeds. The instantaneous volumetric delivery rate $Q_{\text{belt}}(t)$ ($m^3/\text{min}$) is modeled dynamically:
\begin{equation}
    Q_{\text{belt}}(t) = Q_{\text{nominal}} \times \phi_{\text{wear}}(H_{\text{belt}}) \times \psi_{\text{inclination}}(\theta) \times \omega_{\text{weather}}(R)
\end{equation}
Where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $Q_{\text{nominal}}$: Rated factory baseline speed (e.g., $1.0 \; m^3$ per 1.3 minutes).
    \item $\phi_{\text{wear}} \in (0, 1]$: Degradation multiplier reflecting belt surface friction loss and roller tension wear.
    \item $\psi_{\text{inclination}}$: Gravitational resistance factor due to conveyor incline angle $\theta$.
    \item $\omega_{\text{weather}} \in (0, 1]$: Reduction factor due to heavy rain saturation ($R$), causing material slippage or clay adhesion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Closed-Loop Crusher and Screen Directed Graph Engine}
Quarry processing plants operate as a Directed Cyclic Graph (DCG) of crushers ($C_k$) and screens ($F_j$). Oversized aggregate fractions ($D > D_{\text{max}}$) rejected by screen meshes are automatically recirculated back to secondary or tertiary crushers.

The mass flow rate entering screen $j$ at time iteration $t$, denoted as $\dot{m}_{F_j}(t)$, incorporates the circulating load:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{m}_{F_j}(t) = \dot{m}_{\text{primary}}(t) + \sum_{k \in \text{Recirc}} \gamma_k \cdot \dot{m}_{C_k}(t - 1)
\end{equation}
Where $\gamma_k \in [0, 1)$ represents the fraction of oversized stones rejected back to crusher $C_k$.

\subsection{Extensible Machinery Topology and Dynamic Plug-and-Play Engine}
Every machine (crusher, screen, washer, loader, conveyor, haul truck) is abstracted as a **Graph Node Engine**:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Machine Node } \mathcal{M}_e = \Big\langle T_{\text{type}}, \, f_{\text{yield}}(\mathbf{x}), \, \mathbf{P}_{\text{wear}}, \, C_{\text{ops}}(e), \, \mathbf{E}_{\text{out}} \Big\rangle
\end{equation}
Where $T_{\text{type}}$ is machine classification, $f_{\text{yield}}(\mathbf{x})$ is the particle size distribution transfer matrix, $\mathbf{P}_{\text{wear}}$ is the vector of tracked wear parts, $C_{\text{ops}}(e)$ is operational cost, and $\mathbf{E}_{\text{out}}$ defines downstream mass routing edges.

When a site manager adds a new plant component via the web interface, Karry automatically recalculates downstream mass balances, updates maintenance queues, and connects operational logs to financial ledgers without code redeployment.

\subsection{Hybrid Predictive Maintenance Engine and Sensor Fallback Strategy}
Equipment components (jaw liners, mantle pairs, polyurethane screens, engine oil, track pads) wear down based on operational stress. Karry supports three dynamic evaluation metrics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Engine Hours ($\Delta H$)}: Primary for crushers, excavators, and loaders.
    \item \textbf{Odometer Kilometers ($\Delta K$)}: Primary for haul trucks and mobile transport.
    \item \textbf{Calendar Time Fallback (Days / Weeks)}: Used when meters break down (\textit{panne compteur}) or for un-metered assets.
\end{enumerate}

The **Remaining Useful Life (RUL)** for component $p$ under metric $m \in \{\text{Hours}, \text{KM}, \text{Days}\}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    RUL_m(p) = \min \left( \frac{U_{\text{rating}}(p) - U_{\text{accumulated}}(p)}{\bar{u}_{\text{daily}} \times \delta_{\text{abrasion}}}, \; \frac{M_{\text{rating}}(p) - M_{\text{processed}}(p)}{\bar{m}_{\text{daily}} \times \delta_{\text{abrasion}}} \right)
\end{equation}
Where $U_{\text{rating}}$ is rated lifespan in metric $m$, $U_{\text{accumulated}}$ is current usage, $\bar{u}_{\text{daily}}$ is daily usage rate, $M_{\text{processed}}$ is cumulative tonnage, and $\delta_{\text{abrasion}} \ge 1.0$ is the Bond abrasion index multiplier.

\subsubsection{Meter Breakdown Fallback Switch}
If an engine hour or kilometer meter hardware failure occurs, authorized managers activate a **Sensor Fallback Switch**. Karry automatically transitions metric tracking to estimated calendar usage based on the asset's historical 30-day average usage $\bar{u}_{\text{daily}}$, ensuring maintenance alerts continue without interruption.

\subsubsection{Automated Spare Parts Procurement Trigger}
To eliminate unplanned downtime, Karry triggers an automated procurement alert when:
\begin{equation}
    RUL_{\text{days}}(p) \le T_{\text{shipping\_lead\_time}} + \tau_{\text{buffer}}
\end{equation}
Where $T_{\text{shipping\_lead\_time}}$ is transit duration (e.g., 30 days for jaw plates shipped internationally) and $\tau_{\text{buffer}}$ is safety margin stock (7 days by default).

\subsection{Fleet Amortization, Loan Solvency, and Weather Model}
Total hourly operational cost $C_{\text{ops}}(a)$ for heavy asset $a$ is:
\begin{equation}
    C_{\text{ops}}(a) = \left( \frac{C_{\text{capital}} - S_{\text{residual}}}{H_{\text{lifespan}}} \right) + (F_{\text{conso}} \times P_{\text{fuel}}) + W_{\text{operator}} + M_{\text{maintenance}}
\end{equation}

An asset is classified as **Loan Solvent** over financing term $T$ if net operating revenue exceeds debt service obligations:
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{d=1}^{365} \Big( \text{Revenue}_a(d) - \big( C_{\text{ops}}(a) \times \Delta H(d) \big) \Big) > \frac{C_{\text{loan\_annual}}}{T}
\end{equation}

Weather disruptions (e.g., rainy season downpours) reduce working days. Let $N_{\text{total}}$ be total days in month $m$, and $R(m)$ be expected rain stoppage days. Effective daily capacity $P_{\text{eff}}$ and order delivery time $T_{\text{delivery}}$ adjust dynamically:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{eff}}(m) = P_{\text{nominal}} \times \left( 1 - \frac{R(m) + \sigma_{\text{safety}}}{N_{\text{total}}} \right), \quad T_{\text{delivery}} = \frac{Q_{\text{order}}}{P_{\text{eff}}(m)}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Central Warehouse (\textit{Magasin G\'en\'eral}) Network}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Master Inventory Hub}: The \textit{Magasin G\'en\'eral} acts as the primary procurement center, holding high-value spare parts, crusher liners, lubricants, and secondary equipment.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Site Dispatching}: Material dispatches to satellite quarry stores generate digital transfer waybills with automated metric tracking.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inter-Quarry Transfer \& Lifetime Telemetry Retention}
When spare equipment, attachments, or heavy machinery transfer from Warehouse 1 to Warehouse 2:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Metric Clone \& Transfer}: The asset carryover file includes complete historical operating data (accumulated hours/KM, maintenance history, fuel consumption average, accrued depreciation).
    \item \textbf{Continuous Lifetime History}: If the asset returns to Warehouse 1 or moves to a third site, Karry maintains an uninterrupted lifetime timeline of site-specific operating metrics, usage dates, and localized costs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Semi-Annual \& Annual Physical Inventory Audits}
To prevent inventory shrinkage and theft, Karry enforces mandatory physical stock checks (every 6 or 12 months):
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \text{Stock} = \text{Stock}_{\text{physical}} - \left( \text{Stock}_{\text{initial}} + \text{Receipts} - \text{Issues} \right)
\end{equation}
Any variance $\Delta \text{Stock} \neq 0$ triggers an automated internal audit flag identifying responsible store officers.

\subsection{Two-Tier Submission \& Controller Approval Workflow}
Field inputs (meter readings, diesel re-fueling, shift hours, scale tickets) follow a mandatory verification cycle:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Field Entry}: Operator logs shift metrics via PWA. The entry enters a \textbf{Pending Approval} queue.
    \item \textbf{Shift Controller Review}: A designated controller verifies entry validity. The controller can approve, reject, or correct entries.
    \item \textbf{Audit Override Trail}: If a controller modifies an entry (e.g., correcting an inaccurate hour meter reading), Karry logs the controller's identity, timestamp, original value, and modified value in an immutable audit table.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dual-Shift Inter-Shift Validation Protocol ($\Delta H$ Verification)}
Operators log machine metrics at shift changeovers (Day Shift / Night Shift):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shift Start}: Operator logs machine ID, photo timestamp, fuel gauge level, and initial meter $H_{\text{start}}$.
    \item \textbf{Shift End}: Operator logs final meter $H_{\text{end}}$. System computes $\Delta H = H_{\text{end}} - H_{\text{start}}$.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Shift Continuity}: Night shift start meter *must* strictly equal day shift end meter ($H_{\text{start, night}} \equiv H_{\text{end, day}}$). Any mismatch triggers an instant fraud flag on the executive dashboard.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Native PKI Electronic Signature Module}
Karry eliminates paper-based signing, scanning, and emailing. All formal shift handovers, weighbridge tickets, transfer waybills, and controller approvals embed a native **Electronic Signature Capture**:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Touchscreen Canvas Capture}: On-device signature drawing stored as cryptographic vector data.
    \item \textbf{PKI Digital Hash}: Signatures are combined with user credentials, GPS coordinates, and payload SHA-256 hashes, producing tamper-evident digital certificates.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Weighbridge Scale Ticket Security}
Digital scale tickets produce immutable ledger records containing net payload weight, truck registration, timestamp, and weighmaster digital signature. Scale tickets cannot be edited manually; corrections require counter-signed reversal entries.

\subsection{Granular Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)}
System administrators maintain absolute authority over field-level data visibility. Administrators can configure custom Read, Write, and Masked permissions per role (e.g., hiding financial margins from field operators while permitting hour meter entries).

\subsection{Offline-First Web PWA Strategy}
Karry uses a Progressive Web App (PWA) architecture to maintain continuous operation during network outages at isolated site quarries:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Service Workers}: Caches application assets for instant startup without cellular coverage.
    \item \textbf{Background Sync}: Holds offline entries in IndexedDB and automatically syncs data once 4G/Wi-Fi connection is restored.
\end{itemize}
